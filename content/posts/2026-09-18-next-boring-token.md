I've been using LLMs to help write prose, rather than code.

As  discovery tools,  LLMs impressed  me  early on.   For me,  they've
largely replaced Google. And once we  have the context loaded, why not
hand  over the  pen?  Let the  LLM  write the  report  from our  joint
work. In January 2026 that seemed magical. Six months later, rereading
what I'd written, I could  see the embarrassing ChatGPT template style
throughout.

So  I doubled  down and  started  translating the  best writing  style
manuals I know into agentic  skills. That improved the agents' writing
style.  I still  had  to tell  them explicitly  to  stop leading  with
conclusions. 😅

Only then did another problem become clear: the voice could sound like
mine, yet the argument could still contain non sequiturs. And equally
damaging, my opinions get reworded and regressed to the mean. 😱

LLMs generate  text by  predicting the next  token from  the preceding
context. That context can include a plan, but neither the plan nor the
prediction guarantees  that the  next sentence follows  logically from
the  previous   paragraph.   In  my  experience,   the  sentences  can
individually sound  reasonable while  the argument goes  nowhere.  The
writing often feels  linear, devoid of high-level  wit, and remarkably
unremarkable.   A one-line  joke comes  readily; a  short story  whose
setup earns its ending never materialises.

So how can one use LLMs to write prose?

I use  them adversarially. I  tell the agent to  read and give  me its
impression,  leaning  hard on  the  writing  style reference  books  I
adopt. Outside  a work setting,  I can't  rely on another  human being
beside me to review  my writing. With the agent, I  have a reviewer on
tap.

Its  suggestions  I  may  adopt;  its judgement  I  give  very  little
weight. Each  criticism has to  stand on  its own merit.  A convincing
explanation  on the  screen is  no guarantee  of sound  judgement.

The  agent helps  me  out of  writer's freeze.  The  agent reviews  my
grammar. But it will edit me out if I let it.
